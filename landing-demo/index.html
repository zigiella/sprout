Sprout

When network is absent — and the human is far — local criteria still irrigate.

Cuando la red no llega y nadie está cerca, el criterio sigue regando.

Sprout is a local-first AI architecture for water optimization in remote plots — places where the field, the person and the network rarely coincide in time. Three nodes with distinct jurisdictions, an ESP32 safety coprocessor that vetoes physically, and Gemma 4 doing what it does best on each layer. Every intelligence has jurisdiction. And expiry.

Try the three nodes hands-on

Each demo runs against deterministic mocks in your browser — no cloud, no API key, no model download. This is intentional and coherent with the thesis: the real system runs Gemma 4 fully local on Jetson, Pixel and laptop with no network dependency, and we want you to be able to probe the architecture exactly the same way — from any browser, without network. Rhizome

Plot node · Gemma 4 E2B · jurisdiction: minutes

The autonomous brain that lives next to the sensors. Decides offline whether to irrigate, defer, skip or block. Never moves water directly: an ESP32 with custom firmware enforces hard safety limits.

Pollen

Mobile node · Gemma 4 E4B · jurisdiction: visit TTL

The roaming brain on the farmer's Android. Multimodal: feeds raw audio to Gemma 4 directly. Compiles human intent into a MissionPatch that the field can use immediately, federates context between plots without direct network.

Meristem

Slow brain · Gemma 4 E4B · jurisdiction: days

The kitchen-table brain. Receives bundles from Pollen, evaluates with deterministic logic, emits durable policies. The LLM only writes the rationale — the decision is always auditable down to a concrete rule.

Why local-first AI matters here

In 2023, 48% of the world's land area suffered at least one month of extreme drought — the second largest extent since 1951.[1] Drought affects 1.8 billion people and costs $300 billion per year.[1] But the field for which we design doesn't live in continuous connectivity: only 58% of the rural population worldwide uses internet — in low-income countries, 14%.[2]

The bottleneck isn't data. It's the lag between what happens in the plot and the decision that should correct it. When the network fails or the farmer isn't there, that lag is days. Closing it requires moving the decision where the water is — not the other way around.

Sources: [1] UNCCD Global Drought Hotspots 2023-2025 + OECD Global Drought Outlook 2025. [2] ITU Facts and Figures 2025. Full references in research/metrics/impact_stats.md.

How Gemma 4 is used

Sprout uses Gemma 4 in five concrete ways, each exploiting a different capability of the model:

  • Multimodal audio native — Pollen feeds raw 16 kHz .wav to Gemma 4 E4B via LiteRT-LM without a separate STT pipeline. The voice "riega un poco menos, esta planta aguanta más seca de lo que crees" compiles into a structured MissionPatch — in the farmer's pocket, no network. Validated end-to-end against real microphone input.
  • Native tool calling — Meristem uses Gemma 4 E4B with native tool calling for compose_policy(...), validate_bundle(...) and compare_targets(...). The deterministic logic decides; the LLM only writes the rationale. Runtime end-to-end validated with tool_calls_recovered_from_text=1.
  • Three-node routing — three Gemma 4 instances (E2B + E4B + E4B), three jurisdictions, no cloud roundtrip. Model choice is per-node, not global: E2B for direct answers (Rhizome), E4B for tool-calling reasoning (Pollen + Meristem).
  • Bilingual narrator in Rhizome — endpoints GET /summary/since?locale=es|en and GET /explain/decision/<id>?locale=es|en operational in production. Gemma 4 E2B improves the rationale_short over a decision already closed by deterministic logic — never changes action, never authorizes water, never invents facts. The system speaks two languages in production.
  • Approach C i18n localization — official project standard. Edge nodes (Rhizome, ESP32) think in stable English Tech; Pollen + Gemma 4 E4B local translates to the UI language before rendering. Hardware-agnostic, ready for minority-language fine-tuning (Pular, Wolof, Swahili, Quechua, Catalan, Basque).

The final decision is never signed by the LLM. The deterministic Evaluator decides; Gemma 4 writes the rationale and adapts the surface language.

Safety & Trust — the three invariants

Sprout is built around three non-negotiable invariants. Any change to the system has to preserve them:

  1. "Physical layer prevails. Rhizome arbitrates. Pollen mediates. Meristem refines." The ESP32 firmware owns critical sensors and actuators; nothing touches water without its veto. The higher a piece sits in the hierarchy, the less physical authority it has.
  2. "Every intelligence has jurisdiction. And expiry." Every MissionPatch carries a short TTL. Every PolicyPacket carries a validity window. Every WeatherDigest expires before going stale. Expired objects get EXPIRED → REJECTED with legible reason in the DecisionReceipt. No criterion stays silently valid past its window. This rule is what avoids the most common failure of autonomous systems: accepting old orders as if they were new.
  3. No PolicyPacket from Meristem can reduce the firmware's hard limits — it can only recommend more conservative behavior. Authority over physical safety inverts the hierarchy.

Refusal is a feature, not a failure. When the system isn't confident, it says so and asks the human to rephrase or step in. Validated empirically: Pollen rejects agricultural-nonsense sentences with REFUSE_RETRY instead of fabricating an action.
